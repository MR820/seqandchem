Molecular Property Calculator

Calculate molecular weight, formula, logP, TPSA, hydrogen bond donors and acceptors, rotatable bonds, molar refractivity, canonical SMILES, InChI and InChIKey — all from a SMILES or structure file, with no database lookup.

Multiple molecules are allowed — one per line or a multi-record SDF.

What this molecular property calculator does

Paste a SMILES string, MOL, SDF, PDB or InChI structure and the calculator returns a descriptor table for each molecule. Everything is computed from the structure by OpenBabel; there is no external database lookup, so the same input always gives the same values.

The calculator is useful for triaging a compound library, checking a structure before registration, comparing analogues or preparing descriptors for a QSAR model. For a single-purpose calculation, the molecular weight calculator and the Lipinski calculator provide a shorter output.

How the values are calculated

OpenBabel parses the structure, perceives bonds and aromaticity, and applies its descriptor implementations. Molecular weight and formula are deterministic sums of atomic weights. logP and TPSA are predictions based on atom and fragment contributions, not experimental measurements. InChI and InChIKey are generated by the InChI library bundled with OpenBabel.

Because these are predicted values, they can differ from experimental values or from another toolkit's implementation. That is normal. Compare values produced by the same method when you are ranking compounds, and use an experimental measurement when a decision depends on the exact number.

Drug-likeness and the rule of five

The descriptor table contains everything needed to check Lipinski's rule of five: MW ≤ 500, logP ≤ 5, HBD ≤ 5, HBA ≤ 10, plus the common Veber extensions TPSA ≤ 140 Å² and rotatable bonds ≤ 10. The Lipinski calculator applies these thresholds and shows pass/fail for each rule; this page gives the raw values so you can apply your own cutoffs.

Common uses

  • Filtering a compound library by molecular weight, logP or TPSA.
  • Preparing descriptor columns for a QSAR or machine-learning model.
  • Checking a structure before registering it in a database.
  • Comparing analogues during lead optimisation.
  • Generating InChI and InChIKey identifiers for cross-database lookup.

For a library, use the batch converter or call the properties API. For structural comparison, use the Tanimoto similarity calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is logP?

logP is the base-10 logarithm of the octanol/water partition coefficient for the neutral form of a molecule. It measures lipophilicity: higher values mean the compound prefers octanol (fat-like) over water. It is a key input to Lipinski's rule of five.

What is TPSA?

Topological polar surface area is the surface area contributed by polar atoms, mainly nitrogen and oxygen, and their attached hydrogens. It correlates with passive membrane permeability; values above about 140 Å² are often associated with poor oral absorption.

Why do my values differ from PubChem?

Descriptors such as logP and TPSA are predictions, and different implementations use different atom types and correction factors. Molecular weight and formula should match closely; logP and TPSA may differ by a small amount.

Can I calculate properties for multiple molecules?

Yes. Paste one SMILES per line, or upload a multi-record SDF. The result table has one row per molecule. For very large libraries, use the batch endpoint or the API.

Does it handle salts and counter-ions?

Yes, they are included as separate components. That changes the molecular weight and some descriptors. If you want the parent compound only, strip the salt before calculating.

What is molar refractivity?

Molar refractivity is a measure of the total polarizability of a mole of the substance. It is used as a descriptor in QSAR models and is related to molecular volume and polarizability.
