What is a reverse complement?

DNA is double-stranded: each strand is the reverse complement of the other. Given ATGGC on one strand, the opposite strand reads GCCAT in the 5′→3′ direction. The reverse complement operation flips the sequence and swaps every base for its partner (A↔T, G↔C).

Where it is used

  • Designing reverse PCR primers
  • Checking whether a read maps to the forward or reverse strand
  • Analysing genes on the minus strand of a genome

IUPAC ambiguity codes

Ambiguous bases are complemented correctly: R (A/G) ↔ Y (C/T), K (G/T) ↔ M (A/C), S ↔ S, W ↔ W, B ↔ V, D ↔ H, and N stays N.

Frequently asked questions

Does it reverse, complement, or both?

Both — that is the definition of a reverse complement. If you only need the complement, run the tool and reverse the result, or use a sequence editor.
