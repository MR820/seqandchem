Lipinski Rule of Five Calculator

Check whether a molecule is likely to be orally active. Enter a SMILES and the calculator evaluates Lipinski's rule of five plus the Veber extension for polar surface area and flexibility.

Multiple molecules are allowed — one per line.

What is Lipinski's rule of five?

Christopher Lipinski observed in 1997 that most orally administered drugs satisfy four simple physicochemical limits. A compound that violates more than one of them is statistically less likely to reach the market. The "five" comes from the fact that every threshold is a multiple of five.

RuleLimitWhy it matters
Molecular weight≤ 500 DaLarge molecules permeate membranes poorly
logP (lipophilicity)≤ 5Too lipophilic means poor aqueous solubility
Hydrogen bond donors≤ 5Each donor costs energy to desolvate
Hydrogen bond acceptors≤ 10Same, for acceptors

Veber rules

Veber et al. (2002) added two more predictors of oral bioavailability, which this calculator also reports:

  • TPSA ≤ 140 Å² — polar surface area above this usually means poor absorption
  • Rotatable bonds ≤ 10 — more flexibility means more conformational entropy cost

Frequently asked questions

Does passing the rules guarantee a drug?

No. The rules are a cheap filter, not a prediction of efficacy or safety. Many approved drugs violate one rule, and many rule-compliant compounds fail in trials.

Are these rules applied to natural products?

Not reliably. Natural products and PROTACs routinely violate the rule of five yet are still biologically active — the rules were derived from synthetic oral drug libraries.

Where do the numbers come from?

All descriptors are calculated by OpenBabel from the structure alone — no external database or lookup table is involved.
