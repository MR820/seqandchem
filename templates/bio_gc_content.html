What is GC content?

GC content is the percentage of guanine and cytosine bases in a sequence. Because G–C pairs share three hydrogen bonds while A–T pairs share two, GC-rich DNA is more thermally stable. GC content is used to estimate melting temperature, predict PCR success and characterise genomes — bacterial genomes typically sit between 20% and 80% GC.

Other metrics reported

  • Length — number of bases in the record.
  • N count — ambiguous bases, usually assembly gaps.
  • GC skew — (G−C)/(G+C), used to locate origins of replication.
  • AT skew — (A−T)/(A+T), same idea for the other pair.

Frequently asked questions

Does it handle RNA?

Yes — U is counted the same way as T for the AT statistic.

Can I analyse a whole genome?

Yes, as long as the pasted text stays under the input limit. For multi-FASTA files each record is reported separately.
