Molecular Similarity (Tanimoto)

Compare two molecules and get their Tanimoto similarity using FP2, FP3, FP4 or MACCS fingerprints. A score of 1.0 means identical fingerprints, 0.0 means no overlap.

How Tanimoto similarity works

Each molecule is converted into a binary fingerprint — a bit string where each bit means "this substructure is present". The Tanimoto coefficient is the number of shared bits divided by the number of bits present in either molecule:

T = (A ∩ B) / (A ∪ B)

Rules of thumb widely used in cheminformatics: T > 0.85 usually means the same compound, T > 0.5 suggests a similar scaffold, and T < 0.3 indicates little structural overlap.

Which fingerprint should I use?

  • FP2 — Daylight-style path-based fingerprint, good general-purpose choice.
  • FP3 — smaller, faster, more permissive.
  • FP4 — SMARTS-based, emphasises functional groups.
  • MACCS — 166 predefined structural keys, very interpretable.
