What this protein molecular weight calculator does

Paste a protein or peptide sequence in one-letter code or FASTA format. The calculator returns the average molecular weight and the monoisotopic mass in daltons (Da) and kilodaltons (kDa), based on the standard residue masses of the 20 proteinogenic amino acids plus selenocysteine (U) and pyrrolysine (O).

Multiple records are supported: each FASTA entry is calculated independently and its header is preserved. The same calculation applies to a short peptide and a full protein; the only difference is the sequence length.

How the molecular weight is calculated

Each amino acid contributes its residue mass — the mass of the free amino acid minus one water molecule, because a water molecule is lost when the peptide bond forms. The chain has one free N-terminus and one free C-terminus, so one water molecule is added back at the end:

MW = Σ(residue masses) + H₂O

For example, the peptide angiotensin II has the sequence DRVYIHPF. Summing the residue masses of Asp, Arg, Val, Tyr, Ile, His, Pro and Phe and adding 18.015 Da gives 1046.19 Da average mass and 1045.54 Da monoisotopic mass.

Average vs monoisotopic mass

ValueWhat it usesTypical use
Average molecular weightNatural isotopic abundance of each elementSDS-PAGE, protein concentration, general chemistry
Monoisotopic massThe most abundant isotope of each elementHigh-resolution mass spectrometry, peptide mass fingerprinting

The two values differ by a few tenths of a dalton for a short peptide and by tens of daltons for a large protein. Use the monoisotopic mass when comparing with an MS result; use the average mass for gel mobility and general lab calculations. For more detail, see the average vs monoisotopic mass guide.

What is not included

  • Post-translational modifications — phosphorylation, glycosylation, acetylation, methylation and lipidation change the mass; add their mass differences separately.
  • Disulfide bonds — each disulfide bond loses two hydrogen atoms (about 2.016 Da). This calculator reports the reduced, unmodified chain.
  • Signal peptides and propeptides — the sequence you paste is the sequence calculated; remove the signal peptide first if you want the mature protein.
  • Tags and fusion partners — a His-tag, GST or Fc tag adds mass; include it in the sequence if it is part of the construct.
  • Non-standard residues — X, B, Z and J are not resolved. They are counted and reported as unknown; the mass excludes them.

Reading the result

The output shows the sequence length, number of resolved residues, average mass, monoisotopic mass and any unknown codes. If the result says “unknown residues ignored”, fix the sequence or interpret the mass as a lower bound for the resolved portion.

For a protein with a known sequence, a quick sanity check is to compare the result with the mass you expect from the expression system or the vendor. A difference of a few hundred daltons usually means a tag, a signal peptide or a modification is missing from the input.

Common uses

  • Checking a peptide before ordering it from a synthesis service.
  • Estimating the expected band position on an SDS-PAGE gel.
  • Preparing for peptide mass fingerprinting or intact-protein MS.
  • Comparing a construct with and without a tag or fusion partner.
  • Converting a protein sequence into a molar concentration calculation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between average and monoisotopic mass?

Average mass uses the natural abundance of each element; monoisotopic mass uses the most abundant isotope. They are close for small peptides and diverge as the protein gets larger. Use monoisotopic mass for high-resolution MS and average mass for general lab work.

Does it handle FASTA files?

Yes. Paste one or more FASTA records; each record is calculated separately and the header is preserved. A bare sequence without a header is also accepted.

Does it account for disulfide bonds or modifications?

No. The result is the reduced, unmodified chain. Each disulfide bond removes about 2.016 Da; phosphorylation adds about 79.97 Da, acetylation about 42.01 Da, and glycosylation depends on the glycan. Apply those corrections separately.

Why is my mass different from the vendor's value?

Check for a tag, signal peptide, N- or C-terminal modification, non-standard residue or a different isoform. Also confirm whether the vendor reports average or monoisotopic mass.

Can I calculate a peptide mass?

Yes — a peptide is just a short protein. Paste the one-letter sequence and the same residue calculation applies. For a modified peptide, add the modification mass manually.

What do X, B, Z and J mean in my sequence?

They are ambiguity codes: X is any residue, B is Asx, Z is Glx and J is Leu/Ile. The calculator cannot assign a unique mass to them, so it counts them as unknown and excludes them from the reported mass.
